I have learned that starting a student-run newspaper from the ground
up is no easy feat. When starting the newspaper, I explored options
and began emailing teachers, expressing my interest in journalism. I
soon learned that there was a journalism course that was reserved for
seniors— it was solely online and produced a handful of articles each
spring semester. In my sophomore year (2023-2024 academic year), this
course was discontinued due to school funding cuts. I recruited two
friends who expressed interest in writing (along with the possibility
of a paper) and we decided to develop a new, entirely student-run,
newspaper.

<h3>Garnering administrative support</h3>

In the spring of my sophomore year, I connected with the school
administration to explore options. The administration was very
supportive of the work, but acknowledged that there would be no
funding. With an administrative change over the summer, we finalized
the proposal with the new principal in the Fall of 2024.

In addition, the school requires that any school-related activity be
an in-person club with a faculty advisor. As such, we needed to
recruit a faculty member. An English teacher, Justin Chapman, had run
the journalism course in the past and graciously agreed to serve as
our advisor under one condition: that this was entirely student-led.

<h3>Planning</h3>

My two fellow managing editors and I spent the summer after our
sophomore year envisioning what this newspaper could be. (Please refer
to the Design tab for further elaboration). We decided on a print
newspaper, as I had hoped this medium would make the paper more
visible in our community. I wanted the look and feel of a traditional
school newspaper, so I investigated local printing options. However, I
soon learned that local printing was prohibitively expensive, but an
online resource “MakeMyNewspaper” offered both publishing software and
an affordable solution for printing. I secured private donations to
support the work over the first year.

<h3>Recruiting, reporting and publishing</h3>

![Original club-fair slide advertising The Hawks' Nest]({{"/images/c3.png" | relative_url}})

At the start of my junior year, I developed promotional material to
recruit contributors. My fellow managing editors and I actively
recruited staff writers, photographers, and fellow editors at the
school’s annual club fair and publicized our new club through posters
distributed around school, slide shows, the principal’s weekly
newsletter, and through word-of-mouth. Although initially I had to use
my school email, we soon had a standard email for the Editorial Board
to use.

To keep costs down, we decided to limit the production run to four
hundred copies, representing about a fifth to a quarter of our CVU
community of students, faculty and staff. I distinctly remember
receiving the first box of newspaper issues, delivered to my
house. Although I detest getting up any earlier than necessary in the
morning, I eagerly headed to school the following morning to
distribute copies to teachers’ mailboxes to be further disseminated
during Advisory time, as well as distributing them to the library and
front desk. For each issue, I sent a digital copy to our principal who
graciously included it in her weekly report for visibility to the
wider CVU community of family and friends.

<h3>Compilation of issues</h3>

In our first year, we printed six issues, distributing them around the
school and through an online PDF.  Now, one half of the way through
_The Hawks’ Nest_’s second year, we have published and distributed
three new issues with a fourth on the way. Through these endeavors, we
have produced 88 articles total: 61 for year one, 27 so far for year
two. Each issue has a lede and a feature article on the front page. I
designed the issues to routinely have a sports page, an arts page, and
a back page with lighter content such as an advice column. Opinion
pieces and Teacher Features are also common.

<h3>Staffing and Team Development</h3>

In the first year, much of my effort centered on staff recruitment and
encouraging writers to complete reporting and writing in a timely
manner. Early on, I sought to model to staff how to write a proper
article and to fill in significant gaps in content and outreach. To
ensure we had sufficient material, I often ended up writing more often
than intended such that by the end of the first year, I had
contributed nearly half the articles. In our second year, however, we
have been far more successful in writer engagement. A large part of
this is that incoming freshmen learned early on that there was an
established newspaper and thus felt satisfied in joining a robust,
already-existing activity. While we have had a consistent collection
of individual contributors, ranging from seventeen to 21 per issue,
participation has been remarkably different in this second year. As a
result, I have contributed to fewer than one in five articles this
year.  To build confidence and drive engagement, I created training
material and resources for staff writers.

<h3>Course credit</h3>

Although both editors and writers are strengthening their writing
skills with each issue, _The Hawks’ Nest_ has been run as a school
club rather than as a school course. Over the past several months, I
have been working with the Program Director for NEXUS, a personalized
learning program at our high school, to develop a course for future
staff writers and editors to obtain course credit for their
work. Working with the Program Director, I am planning out multiple
layers of course credit to optimize flexibility and engagement. I am
also in the process of writing a guidance document that will serve as
a course syllabus for future contributors.

<h3>Editing</h3>

In our first year, I directly edited each article that was published
(around sixty articles). In our second year, a staff editor has
additionally contributed to the process of editing. During our club
time, the staff editor and I are paired with staff writers to get
articles completed more quickly and efficiently. Earlier submissions
are generally done by our staff editor, and I field articles closer to
the deadline. Google Docs serves as our mechanism for sharing writing
and communicating edits. After editing, stories are imported into our
publishing software where I perform last-minute copyediting.

<h3>Funding</h3>

Funding has been an ongoing issue for _The Hawks’ Nest_. Our private
donations fully covered the first year; however, we aimed to be
self-sufficient in our second year. In the spring of our paper’s first
year, we recruited a business manager. One of our managing editors
also was able to sell an advertisement from a local business, which
covered the cost of one issue. With the help of our faculty advisor
and business manager, we were able to run a Winter Auction in which we
fundraised nearly three issues worth of funding (see Marketing and
Audience Engagement section). I have applied for two grants from a
local resource but have not been successful in garnering funds as
yet. Future discussions on the sustainability of our current model
will be warranted for the years ahead.
