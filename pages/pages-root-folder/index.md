<h1>Personal Narrative</h1>

When I entered freshman year, I recognized there was something missing
from the extracurricular activities offered: we didn’t have a school
newspaper and hadn’t had one in any real form since the late
‘90s. This was surprising, given I attend the largest high school in
the state.

After a year and a half—and much convincing of fellow classmates—we
managed to publish the first issue of our new school paper (_The Hawks’
Nest_) in October of 2024. During these months, I learned about the
ins-and-outs of the school’s systems and the requirements of
publishing a paper. After pitching my idea to the administration, I
identified a place to publish print issues, started a club and
recruited members, and worked with the staff to get the newspaper off
the ground. Though I am particularly drawn to editing, my efforts
extended well beyond the usual functions of an editor-in-chief.

I am proud of what this platform has brought to the school itself. It
has been rewarding to watch each staff writer identify their own way
to showcase their interests, including covering sports updates and
writing editorials on the environmental impact of driving to school. I
have been fortunate to see our articles inspire local news coverage on
the history of journalism in local schools and impact on the community
through discussion on school policy. Though the work has been
difficult, it has been particularly satisfying to hold a physical copy
of the paper in my hands when all is said and done.

![Opening up the box containing our first issues of The Hawks' Nest from October 2024.]({{"/images/newspaper.png" | relative_url}}){: style="float: left; margin-right: 1em;"}
<figcaption>Opening up the box containing our first issues of <i>The Hawks' Nest</i> from October 2024.</figcaption>
<br>

Looking back on the past one and a half years, I am grateful for what
our small team has achieved. We started a newspaper that engaged
nearly twenty contributors per issue, fundraised, created recruitment
and training documents, and ended up writing almost one hundred
articles over nine print issues. We earned a school Inspiration Award
in Fall 2024, an award given to students by staff who feel they
deserve recognition. We are currently developing a mechanism for
future writers to earn course credit, collaborating with our local
newspaper to allow students to publish in a professional paper, and
discussing how to have a digital presence.

![Our Editorial Board winning a school Inspiration Award in the Fall of 2024]({{"/images/inspiration.png" | relative_url}}){: style="float: left; margin-right: 1em;"}
<figcaption>Our editorial board winning a school Inspiration Award.</figcaption>
<br>

In the words of the nominator, our faculty advisor, Justin Chapman:
"When they approached me last year and asked whether I might sponsor a
newspaper, I was extremely skeptical. I said to myself, sure, let's
see where this goes, but I'll be surprised if anything comes of it. I
hesitate to admit to you now. However, imagine my surprise when a week
ago they produced 400 copies of the first print journalism at CVU in
at least six or seven years, if not ten. So the fact that they single
handedly resurrected print journalism is just amazing to me and I'd
like to, they give me hope for the future and they inspire me truly."

Beyond the early experiences in journalism, I feel I have personally
learned skills that can be transferred to later academic and
extracurricular pursuits. Not only has it improved my communication
and writing skills, it has also helped me lead and delegate, work
under pressure, help students voice their opinions, fundraise and
manage a budget, and hopefully has prepared me well for future
endeavors.

More than anything actually published in the paper, I feel its
existence is proof that we students can contribute respectfully to the
community’s civil and political discourse. Getting students to both
produce and consume unbiased media has become especially important in
today’s society with the rise of artificial intelligence and
misinformation. Publications such as school newspapers allow us to
share our perspectives, self-advocate, and be further engaged in our
world.

<h1>About Me</h1>

![Grace Warrington]({{"/images/forehead.png" | relative_url}}){: style="float: left; margin-right: 1em;"}
<figcaption></figcaption>

Unsurprisingly, many of my hobbies also have to do with words and language. 

I construct crossword puzzles professionally and have contributed to
newspapers such as _The Washington Post_, _The Los Angeles Times_, and
_The Wall Street Journal_, along with over 400 others through
syndication. To my understanding, I am the youngest female constructor
to publish in _The New York Times_ (by one month).

This love of words has been the inspiration of my passion for
crosswords; my interests in Latin, Classics, and the English language;
my initiative in starting a school newspaper; and the joy I find in
reading. It is also physically evident in my needlessly large
collection of dictionaries, thesauri, and other reference works.
