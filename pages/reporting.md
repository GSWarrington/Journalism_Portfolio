As part of _The Hawks’ Nest_ newspaper club, staff writers and editors
meet weekly to discuss topics and events and assign coverage. Since
_The Nest_’s inception, I have served as Editor-in-chief. In this
role, I have tried to defer to staff writers or other editors in
reporting and writing whenever possible. I often cover agreed-upon
topics that other writers have not seen as priority or that had
coverage fall through. In this capacity, I have written on a wide
range of topics since _The Hawks’ Nest_’s first issue. I have covered
sports updates, such as “CVU's First Junior Varsity Dance Team,”
“Winter Sports in Brief,” and “The April Unified Basketball Game.” I
have reported on many art events such as theater performances
(“Highlights From the Footloose Musical,” “CVU's 2025 Senior One-Act
Play Program kicks off, and CVU Stages Into the Woods,” and “Senior
One-Acts”), and art recognition events (Photo Club contests, “Poetry
Out Loud,” “Students Selected for All-State Musical Festival,”
“Scholastic Art and Writing Awards,” and “CVU Introduces Unified Art
Program”).

Occasionally, I cover short features, often only a paragraph or two,
in which I do not list a byline. To recognize the community and its
members, I have written several spotlights on faculty and staff (“An
Interview With Our New Principal,” “Beloved Head Football Coach is
Stepping Down From Role,” “Teacher Feature: Interview with Charlie
MacFadyen,” Teacher Feature: Cameron Brownell,” “Interviews With
Retiring Teachers,” and “John Bennett After Retirement”).

My favorite piece, however, was a two-part investigation on the
history of the school newspaper at our high school, “The Fine Print: A
History of CVU’s Printed Newspaper.” I collaborated with one of my
managing editors on this piece, with myself writing the first half and
her writing the second section on more recent papers. Initially, I
wrote nearly half of the articles in our newspaper, averaging 4.6
articles per issue. For the second year, I wrote less than 20% of the
articles, averaging 1.6 articles per issue. This change reflects our
success in recruiting staff writers and the development of our paper
(see the section on Editing, Leadership and Team Building).

<h4>Sample articles</h4>

* Volume I, Issue 4: <a href="https://gswarrin.w3.uvm.edu/Grace_Warrington_Journalism_Portfolio/dance-teams.pdf">“CVU's first Junior Varsity Dance Team,” pages 6, 7</a><br>

  This article features the Junior Varsity Dance Team and marks the
  start of the first official dance team at our high school. This is
  one example of where the newspaper helps to showcase parts of our
  local community.

* Volume I, Issue 5: <a href="https://gswarrin.w3.uvm.edu/Grace_Warrington_Journalism_Portfolio/fine-print.pdf">“The Fine Print: A History of CVU’s Printed Newspaper,” pages 1, 3-5</a> <br>

  This article is the first of two installments about the history of
  school newspapers at CVU. The article concerns the paper through its
  existence as The Clarion, which ran from 1964-1972, and the Cow
  Valley Press, which ran from 1972-1978/9. _The Hawks’ Nest_ merely
  follows in the steps of many like minds before ours.

* Volume I, Issue 6: <a href="https://gswarrin.w3.uvm.edu/Grace_Warrington_Journalism_Portfolio/interviews.pdf">“Interviews With Retiring Teachers,” pages 4, 5</a><br>

  This article is an extension of one standard element contained in
  _The Hawks’ Nest_—the “Teacher Feature.” This particular piece
  highlights several retiring teachers.

* Volume II, Issue 1: <a href="https://gswarrin.w3.uvm.edu/Grace_Warrington_Journalism_Portfolio/eic.pdf">“Letter from the Editor-in-chief,” page 1</a><br>

  This article introduces _The Hawks’ Nest_ for its second year. It is
  intended to provide a public voice for the newspaper and to showcase
  our efforts to new members of the community.

* Volume II, Issue 2: <a href="https://gswarrin.w3.uvm.edu/Grace_Warrington_Journalism_Portfolio/unified.pdf">“CVU Introduces Unified Art Program,” page 5</a><br>

  According to Missy Shiner Shea, CEO of Special Olympics Vermont,
  Unified Sports “involves students with intellectual disabilities who
  we call athletes and students without intellectual disabilities who
  we call partners.” This year, our high school introduced a Unified
  Arts Program, which is a sister program to the Unified Sports
  Program. This article is meant to widen awareness for the program.
