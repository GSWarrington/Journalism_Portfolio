The “Vermont Statutes Title 16. Education, § 180, Student
Rights—Freedom of Expression” law allows for substantial freedom for
student journalists. Therefore, students can not be censored or
disciplined for journalistic endeavors that do not cause obvious harm
to others in the community.

Beyond the law, however, I have felt very supported and protected in
my journalistic freedoms by the administration, faculty, and
staff. Both faculty advisors have provided remarkable latitude in this
work. Although, at various times, both advisors have asked to see the
slugs (preview articles), they have not requested significant changes
to content. We have been able to cover controversial topics without
reprimand, such as questioning school policies and discussing national
politics.

In fact, I have instead reached out for guidance on rare occasions
when I have been unclear what is the best course of action to continue
to respect student privacy and maintain journalistic integrity. On one
such occasion, we had a medical incident at a community event. Some
staff members wanted to cover the event fully, but others felt privacy
should be maintained. The school principal and faculty advisor were
immensely helpful in providing me with language to navigate the
incident. As a product of working together, I feel there is trust and
mutual respect in this endeavor.

Although _The Hawks’ Nest_’s staff has been very aware of the need to
respect our community, I work hard in the editing process to ensure
that language is respectively delivered and that there is nothing
obscene or slanderous. In addition, I work to ensure quotes are
properly attributed and appropriately sourced. On occasion, we have
cited anonymous sources, with permission of the individual providing
the quote. In a small community, we also have worked to ensure the
appropriate pronoun is used in our coverage or have preferentially
used names over pronouns when this information is not known. Any
needed corrections are covered in the subsequent issue.

While obtaining photographic coverage of community events has required
some resourcefulness, the effort does not end there. As some students
have opted not to sign the school’s photo release waiver, I have
needed to follow up with images that capture student faces in an
identifiable way. Occasionally, I have blurred faces if I have been
unable to get this information in a timely manner or have changed the
page layout to accommodate this requirement. I have also followed up
with each image to ensure that appropriate credit is obtained. Photo
credits are listed under images and individuals are also credited in
the masthead.

I am currently working to obtain course credit for future editors and
staff writers for participation in _The Hawks’ Nest_. I am hopeful that
this future curriculum can more thoroughly explore the legal and
ethical responsibilities of student journalism.
