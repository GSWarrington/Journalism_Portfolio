Photography and the use of images to tell stories are essential parts
of a newspaper. Since recruitment of a Photography Editor and
consistent staff photographers has proved elusive, I have served as
the principal individual to solicit for images since _The Hawks’ Nest_’s
inception last year. My own skill as a photographer is limited, as
evidenced by the sole photograph I took for the newspaper. In my
defense I was rushed.

![]({{"/images/cafeteria.png" | relative_url}})
<figcaption></figcaption>
<br>

Instead, I have solicited 104 photographs and thirty images from
community members, students, faculty, and staff over each of the
paper’s nine issues. Through these issues, I have worked to tell each
individual story through the photos chosen, tailoring the selection to
convey specific meanings.

For example, by placing a photograph of the entrance of the school on
the front page of our first issue, we were, in effect, welcoming the
Champlain Valley Union High School (CVU) community to _The Hawks’ Nest_.

![]({{"/images/cvu.png" | relative_url}})
<figcaption></figcaption>
<br>

In another example, we captured the celebratory nature of the winter’s
Snow Ball by using an inverted the photograph of the scene.

As many outlets celebrated the national championship win of the CVU
Girl’s Cross Country team, I selected images that showed the fun and
personality of the runners rather than through traditional angles.

![]({{"/images/xcountry.png" | relative_url}})
<figcaption></figcaption>
<br>

In the absence of a team of photographers, I occasionally reach out to
the school’s photography club or other artistic activities to support
community involvement.

Our most common photojournalistic coverage, however, was of community
events. I was able to obtain images to report on many meaningful
milestones across the school including community events such as the
Empty Bowls’ fundraiser for the local food shelf or our theater
performances.

During my time as Editor-in-chief I have been aware that, in addition
to describing current events, a school newspaper serves as a record of
the school’s community. The image of the American flag that spans a
dark hallway in our school may serve as a vital record of the
soon-to-be destroyed 1992 school mural in years to come.

![]({{"/images/flag.png" | relative_url}})
<figcaption></figcaption>
<br>

By connecting with alumni from the 1970s, I was able to retrieve
copies of the school newspapers from the school’s beginnings fifty
years ago. These efforts enhanced my school’s own records and would
have been lost to the community without the effort. We captured images
from old papers in two of our issues and were able to showcase old
printing techniques through this effort. The digital scans we made of
each issue are now available to the wider school community.
