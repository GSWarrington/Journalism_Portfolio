While producing nine print issues is no easy task, my managing editors
and I have felt immense freedom in designing a newspaper from the
ground up. Quite gratifyingly, every decision and option has been
ours. During our sophomore summer, the two managing editors and I
gathered several times to consider what type of newspaper we wanted
our high school to have. Here is a short list of some of our early
decisions oscillating between the trivial and the important: The name,
logos, fonts, number of columns across the page, leading, where the
apostrophe should reside in _The Hawks’ Nest_, the publishing format,
how many pages, how many copies should be printed, standardized
sections, critical staff positions, the frequency, how individuals
should be referred to (class year is listed wherever relevant; first
reference to individuals are bolded), caption font size, how and when
to give credit, and which style guide to follow (New York Times), just
to name a few. An important detail is in the newspaper’s name—_The
Hawks’ Nest_. The paper’s name is in deference to the school’s mascot,
the “red hawk.” I advocated for having the apostrophe after the plural
(Hawks’ versus Hawk’s) to recognize all the individuals within our
community as opposed to just one.

As a result of our conversations and the formation of our Editorial
Board, we determined which direction we wanted to head going
forward. This included it being printed through MakeMyNewspaper, what
the standard template of our paper looked like, which standard
sections we wanted to include (News, Arts, Sports, and Opinion), and
the need for photographic and business expertise and a faculty
advisor.

* **News:** The managing editors and I have directed staff writers to
    cover upcoming events and through unexpected developments within
    the school. However, they are given a lot of leeway with which
    subjects they would like to cover, as I feel it’s important that
    the author is interested in the topic they are writing. With a
    recognized need for editorial support, I was able to recruit a
    staff editor position in the 2025-2026 school year to help edit
    articles.

* **Arts:** We have not had a formal Arts Editor to date, but I hope
    this could be a future position in years to come. I have often
    served as Arts Editor, writing and editing reviews of band
    concerts, chorus recitals, play productions, and art
    shows. Outside of journalistic content, common sources of art
    include: freelance illustrations, comic strips, and our work with
    the photography club. _The Hawks’ Nest_ has three logos, all created
    by a managing editor—one, an inkwell; two, a seal with the
    rolled-up newspapers; and three, the title font.

* **Sports:** We have been very lucky to have one very engaged Sports
    Editor in our first year along with second this year.

* **Editorial:** While not a formal position, we have an excellent
    engagement in editorials and staff writers often elect to do them
    each month.

* **Business manager:** This has been an essential position in our
    newspaper and we hope to increase to several more positions within
    the next few years to fully support the paper’s need for funding.

* **Faculty advisor:** We are grateful to have had two volunteer
    faculty advisors over the past two years—an initial English
    teacher who provided us with remarkable autonomy and support and a
    Speech Language Pathologist this year who has helped us run
    meetings and lead the fundraiser activities.

* **Crossword puzzles:** Near and very dear to my heart: every issue
    of _The Hawks’ Nest_ has had a crossword puzzle. Grids tend to range
    10x10-12x12 and answers for each month are printed upside down on
    the back page. Each grid aims for a lively fill, occasionally
    referencing aspects of the school or our state in the clues. I am
    creating a crossword puzzle archive of approximately twenty 10x10
    puzzles in order to continue the tradition in _The Hawks’ Nest_ past
    my tenure as Editor-in-chief. This will be shared with the Faculty
    Advisor and successive Editor-in-chief.
